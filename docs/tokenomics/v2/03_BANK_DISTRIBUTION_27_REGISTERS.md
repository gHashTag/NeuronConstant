## Abstract

TRI (TRINITY) has a fixed, deterministic total supply of **7,625,597,484,987 TRI** — equal to 3²⁷, the number of leaves in a depth-27 balanced ternary tree. Every token must be earned through chip-attested ZK proofs; no pre-mine, no airdrop, no vesting cliff exists anywhere in the supply schedule. This document specifies the exact mapping of all 7.6 trillion TRI across 27 ternary registers (t0–t26), partitioned into three banks that correspond to the three branches of the TRI-27 kernel: Bank T (Compute), Bank R (Runtime Mining Pool), and Bank S (Sacred Public Goods).

The 27-register structure is not an arbitrary design decision. It derives directly from the ternary arithmetic at the heart of the TRI-27 architecture: 3 banks × 9 registers each = 27 = 3³. Every allocation boundary is therefore a power of three, making the supply schedule auditable from first principles.

## 1 — The TRI-27 Kernel and its 27 Registers

### 1.1 Why 27?

The TRI-27 chip operates on a ternary logic substrate. Its kernel is a depth-27 balanced ternary Merkle tree. Each of the 27 leaf slots corresponds to one "register" in the supply schedule: a dedicated sub-pool of TRI that can only be unlocked by a specific class of ZK proof generated by, or attested through, a TRI-27 chip.

The number 27 emerges naturally:

```
3^1  =     3   (trit)
3^2  =     9   (tryte)
3^3  =    27   registers  ← kernel leaf count
3^27 = 7,625,597,484,987  ← total TRI supply
```

Each register ti is a leaf of the kernel tree. The three banks are the three subtrees rooted at depth 1:

```
Root (3^27 TRI)
├── Bank T  — t0…t8   (depth-1 left child)
├── Bank R  — t9…t17  (depth-1 middle child)
└── Bank S  — t18…t26 (depth-1 right child)
```

### 3.3 Register t0 — B5 ZK Job Prover (25 %)

**Allocated:** 1,906,399,371,247 TRI  
**Proof module:** B5 — ZK Job Prover  
**Weight in Bank T:** ~41.7 %

The ZK Job Prover is the highest-weight register in the entire supply schedule, reflecting that the primary value proposition of the TRINITY network is trustless, chip-attested proof-of-work for arbitrary compute jobs. A chip earns from t0 by:

1. Accepting a compute job from the network job queue.
2. Executing the job on-chip, generating a PLONK/STARK proof of correct execution.
3. Submitting the proof to the B5 verifier contract on-chain.
4. Having the proof verified within a single consensus epoch.

TRI is minted proportionally to verified proof weight (measured in gate-equivalents) up to the epoch ceiling. No job completion without a verifiable proof earns any reward.

### 3.4 Register t1 — B2 Bandwidth Attestation (8 %)

**Allocated:** 610,047,798,799 TRI  
**Proof module:** B2 — Bandwidth Attestation  
**Weight in Bank T:** ~13.3 %

Network throughput is a first-class resource in the TRINITY economy. Register t1 rewards chip operators who route and attest real data flows across the network fabric. Earning mechanism:

1. The TRI-27 chip measures actual bytes routed through its network interface over a fixed time window (one epoch = one ternary block period).
2. It generates a bandwidth attestation proof: a ZK statement that commits to (a) the measured byte count, (b) the chip's authenticated identifier, (c) the epoch nonce from the consensus beacon.
3. The proof is submitted to the B2 verifier. Reward is linear in attested bandwidth up to the per-epoch per-chip cap.

The cap prevents a single node from monopolising the register. Bandwidth attestations that are not accompanied by corresponding routing receipts from at least two independent peer chips are rejected.

### 3.5 Register t2 — B7 PoRep Storage (8 %)

**Allocated:** 610,047,798,799 TRI  
**Proof module:** B7 — Proof of Replication (PoRep)  
**Weight in Bank T:** ~13.3 %

Persistent, verifiable storage is essential for the network's state layer. Register t2 rewards chip operators running storage nodes that prove continuous, retrievable, unique copies of network data. Earning mechanism:

1. The chip seals a data sector using the B7 sealing function, producing a commitment unique to the chip's identity and the data content.
2. A PoRep proof is generated on-chip and submitted at each storage epoch.
3. Continuous storage is verified via periodic PoSt (Proof of Space-Time) challenges. Missing a challenge window results in a penalty deducted from pending rewards before they vest.

Register t2 mirrors t1 in weight, reflecting the symmetric importance of storage and bandwidth to a functional distributed compute network.

### 3.6 Register t3 — B9 Bittensor Validator Attestations (10 %)

**Allocated:** 762,559,748,499 TRI  
**Proof module:** B9 — Bittensor Validator Interface  
**Weight in Bank T:** ~16.7 %

Register t3 bridges the TRI-27 chip economy with the Bittensor AI subnet ecosystem. TRI-27 chips that operate as Bittensor validators — scoring miner responses and updating the metagraph — earn from t3 by generating chip-attested validator activity proofs. Earning mechanism:

1. The chip participates in a Bittensor subnet as a registered validator.
2. At each scoring epoch, it generates a ZK attestation covering: (a) the set of miner UIDs scored, (b) the assigned weights, (c) a commitment to the model/scoring logic version, (d) the Bittensor block hash.
3. The attestation is submitted to the B9 verifier contract. The contract cross-checks that the chip's on-chain validator key matches its attested chip identity.

This register gives TRI-27 a direct monetary stake in improving Bittensor subnet quality, creating aligned incentives between chip operators and AI subnet health.

### 3.7 Register t4 — B8 DID Personhood (3 %)

**Allocated:** 228,767,924,550 TRI  
**Proof module:** B8 — Decentralised Identity / Personhood  
**Weight in Bank T:** ~5.0 %

Sybil resistance is a precondition for fair distribution. Register t4 rewards unique human participants who establish verifiable on-chain personhood via the B8 DID module. Earning mechanism:

1. A participant completes the chip-attested identity ceremony: a ZK proof that the presenter controls a unique biometric credential bound to a single TRI-27 chip, without revealing the biometric data itself.
2. The B8 verifier issues a non-transferable personhood credential linked to the chip's DID.
3. Holding an active personhood credential grants access to a small per-epoch baseline reward from t4, acting as a Sybil-resistant universal participation incentive.

Register t4 is intentionally small (3 %) to prevent gaming: the credential earns a modest baseline, not a dominant share of supply. The design assumption is one chip per person maximum; chip cloning is detectable via timing-based ZK proofs.

### 3.8 Register t5 — B4 Mesh Router Relay (2 %)

**Allocated:** 152,511,949,700 TRI  
**Proof module:** B4 — Mesh Router  
**Weight in Bank T:** ~3.3 %

Register t5 incentivises the physical-layer mesh connectivity that B4-class chips provide. Mesh routers extend TRINITY network coverage into areas without existing internet infrastructure. Earning mechanism:

1. The chip operates in router mode, forwarding packets between two or more peers that have no direct IP path.
2. It generates a relay proof covering: forwarded packet hashes, upstream and downstream chip identifiers, relay latency measurements.
3. Proofs are batched and submitted to the B4 verifier. Reward is proportional to unique peer pairs relayed, weighted by the geographic diversity of those pairs.

Geographic diversity weighting is computed from chip-attested GPS coordinates sealed in hardware at manufacturing time. This prevents a single operator from running a cluster of co-located chips and claiming relay rewards without providing real geographic coverage.

### 3.9 Register t6 — B3 RPKI BGP Signing (1 %)

**Allocated:** 76,255,974,850 TRI  
**Proof module:** B3 — Resource Public Key Infrastructure / BGP  
**Weight in Bank T:** ~1.7 %

BGP route origin hijacking is one of the most persistent threats to internet routing integrity. Register t6 rewards chip operators who sign BGP route origin attestations using the RPKI framework, anchoring internet routing security to chip-level ZK proofs. Earning mechanism:

1. The chip holds an RPKI certificate covering one or more IP prefixes it originates.
2. At each epoch, it generates a ZK proof that it has signed valid Route Origin Authorisations (ROAs) for its prefixes, without revealing the private signing key.
3. The B3 verifier confirms the proof against the publicly visible RPKI repository.

This register is small (1 %) because the pool of legitimate BGP operators is narrow. Its purpose is to make RPKI participation economically rational for autonomous system operators who adopt the TRI-27 stack.

### 3.10 Register t7 — B6 GKR Sumcheck (2 %)

**Allocated:** 152,511,949,700 TRI  
**Proof module:** B6 — GKR Sumcheck Protocol  
**Weight in Bank T:** ~3.3 %

The GKR sumcheck protocol enables efficient verification of layered arithmetic circuit evaluations — the core operation in many ZK proof systems. Register t7 rewards chip operators who run GKR prover services, accelerating the proof generation pipeline for the broader network. Earning mechanism:

1. The chip accepts GKR prover jobs from the network: a layered circuit description and an input vector.
2. It generates the GKR sumcheck transcript on-chip and returns it to the requester along with a proof of correct transcript generation.
3. The proof is logged to the B6 verifier contract. Reward is proportional to circuit depth × input size, subject to the per-epoch cap.

Register t7 is a specialist register intended for chips with high-throughput arithmetic coprocessors. It creates a market for ZK acceleration services within the TRINITY network itself.

### 3.11 Register t8 — Champion BPB Bounties (1 %)

**Allocated:** 76,255,974,850 TRI  
**Proof module:** Champion — Best Proven Benchmark (BPB)  
**Weight in Bank T:** ~1.7 %

Register t8 is the competitive record register. It releases TRI to chip operators who establish and hold the verified Best Proven Benchmark (BPB) for a designated performance category: fastest proof generation, lowest latency relay, highest storage throughput, etc. The 2.2393 figure refers to the current record ratio (best-in-class / theoretical minimum) that defines the bounty threshold.

Earning mechanism:

1. A chip submits a benchmark proof claiming a new record for a designated category.
2. A 72-hour challenge window opens. Any other chip may submit a counter-proof. The challenge is resolved on-chain by the B5/B6/B7 verifier relevant to the category.
3. The record holder receives a continuous stream of t8 emissions until their record is beaten.
4. When a record is broken, a settlement transaction transfers earned-but-unvested t8 rewards to the previous record holder, and the new record holder begins accruing.

This register rewards long-run excellence, not just participation. The 72-hour challenge window prevents flash-in-the-pan claims.

## 4 — Bank R: RUNTIME Mining Pool (t9–t17)

### 4.1 Overview

Bank R holds **2,287,679,245,496 TRI** — thirty percent of total supply. It functions as the long-run emission smoothing mechanism, analogous to a block subsidy reserve in a proof-of-work chain. All tokens in Bank R are released through chip-attested mining; the difference from Bank T is temporal rather than mechanical: Bank R tokens are released according to a halving schedule across nine epochs, ensuring that mining incentives persist even as the Bank T pool depletes.

### 4.2 Design Rationale: Equal Split vs. Weighted Allocation

Two allocation designs were evaluated for t9–t17:

**Option A — Equal split (3.333… % per register):**  
Each register receives 1/9 of Bank R = 254,186,582,833 TRI. Combined with a standard halving (each epoch emits half of the remaining pool), this gives a smooth geometric decay: total per-epoch emissions halve every epoch, providing predictable long-run supply curves.

**Option B — Weighted (decreasing front-loaded):**  
Earlier registers receive more; later ones less. This rewards early miners more aggressively and may accelerate network bootstrapping, but it front-loads inflation and makes the long-run supply curve more complex.

**Recommendation: Option A (equal split).**  
Reasons: (1) Simplicity — every auditor can verify the schedule from a single formula. (2) Neutrality — no epoch cohort is privileged over another. (3) Alignment with ternary symmetry — 9 equal parts preserves the ternary structure of the bank. (4) The halving schedule already provides front-loading at the emission level; additional weighting would compound it unnecessarily.

### 4.4 Halving Epoch Structure

Within each halving epoch ti (i ∈ {9, …, 17}), the 254,186,582,833 TRI is released over a series of ternary block periods. The per-block reward is set so that the epoch pool is fully exhausted by the epoch's end. Epoch length is defined in ternary blocks (3ⁿ blocks per epoch, where n is chosen by governance at network launch based on target block time).

The per-block reward formula within epoch i:

```
reward_per_block(i) = pool(i) / blocks_per_epoch(i)
```

Because pool(i) is constant across all epochs (equal split), and blocks_per_epoch(i) doubles each epoch (standard halving), the per-block reward halves each epoch:

```
epoch 1: pool / B blocks
epoch 2: pool / 2B blocks → half the per-block reward
epoch 3: pool / 4B blocks → quarter the per-block reward
...
epoch 9: pool / 256B blocks → 1/256th the per-block reward
```

This produces a smooth, auditable, predictable tail emission that keeps chip operators incentivised across a span of approximately 3 generations of chip hardware.

### 4.5 Eligibility for Bank R Rewards

All nine B-module proof classes (B2–B9, plus BPB champion) are eligible to earn from whichever Bank R epoch register is currently active. The split within each epoch among active proof classes follows the same weighting as Bank T (i.e., ZK Job Prover proofs receive the largest share within each epoch, proportionally). This means Bank R is not a separate mining class — it is a temporal reserve that extends Bank T incentives forward in time.

### 5.3 Register t18 — DARPA / Defence Partnership Rewards (3 %)

**Allocated:** 228,767,924,550 TRI  
**Domain:** Strategic defence and intelligence research partnerships

Register t18 funds contributions arising from formal partnerships with defence research agencies (DARPA and equivalents). This is the largest Bank S register, reflecting the strategic importance of sovereign compute security for national-critical infrastructure.

**Earning path:**

1. A formal partnership agreement is established between the TRINITY Foundation and an eligible defence research agency. The agreement is published as a governance proposal and ratified by TRI token holders.
2. Deliverables are specified in the agreement as verifiable artefacts: software modules, research reports, hardware specifications, or formal proofs.
3. Each deliverable is submitted via a chip-attested commit: the contributor's TRI-27 chip signs the deliverable hash, generating a ZK proof that the signing chip identity matches the registered partnership contributor DID.
4. The t18 verifier contract checks the proof and releases the agreed TRI allocation upon delivery confirmation.

**Anti-gaming note:** Partnership agreements are time-locked for a minimum of 90 days from ratification before any t18 tokens can be claimed. This prevents fabricated last-minute partnerships.

### 5.4 Register t19 — Academic Coq Verification Grants (2 %)

**Allocated:** 152,511,949,700 TRI  
**Domain:** Formal verification — Coq proof library contributions

Register t19 funds contributors who publish formally verified proofs of TRINITY protocol components using the Coq theorem prover (or equivalent formal verification systems: Lean, Isabelle/HOL). Formal verification of cryptographic and consensus protocols is a public good with large positive externalities.

**Earning path:**

1. A contributor formalises a component of the TRINITY protocol — a ZK circuit, a consensus rule, a cryptographic primitive — in Coq.
2. The Coq file is submitted to the TRINITY formal verification repository under a peer-review PR workflow.
3. A minimum of two independent reviewers with registered TRI-27 chip DIDs must approve the PR.
4. Upon merge, the contributor's chip generates a chip-attested commit proof linking the Coq file hash to the contributor's DID.
5. The t19 verifier releases TRI proportional to the verified circuit complexity (measured in proof obligations).

**Peer-review requirement:** No single-author approval. The two-reviewer requirement means Bank S academic grants are gate-kept by the community, not by the Foundation alone.

**Scope:** Only PRs to explicitly designated gHashTag repositories earn from t20. Contributions to forks, unaffiliated repos, or private repositories do not qualify. The list of designated repositories is maintained as an on-chain registry updatable by governance.

**Allocated:** 76,255,974,850 TRI

### 5.9 Registers t24–t26 — Reserved (Future Use)

**Allocated:** 25,418,658,283 TRI each (≈ 0.333 % each; total ≈ 1 %)

Registers t24, t25, and t26 are locked at network genesis. No tokens can be minted from these registers until:

1. A governance proposal specifying a new public goods use case is submitted.
2. The proposal includes a fully specified ZK proof module for the new earning mechanism.
3. The proposal passes with a supermajority vote of active chip DID holders.
4. The proof module undergoes a 30-day public audit period with no critical findings.

**Candidate future uses (non-exhaustive):**
- Hardware security bounties (chip-level vulnerability disclosures).
- Climate / energy reporting (chip-attested renewable energy sourcing proofs).
- Interoperability standards contributions (formal specs for cross-chain ZK bridge protocols).

The reservation of exactly three registers (one per ternary digit of the last bank tryte) maintains the ternary symmetry of the supply schedule even before these registers are activated.

## 7 — Anti-Gaming Measures

### 7.1 Chip Identity Binding

Every TRI-27 chip has a unique, hardware-sealed identity (chip DID) generated during manufacturing via a Physical Unclonable Function (PUF). The chip DID is embedded in every ZK proof the chip generates as a non-forgeable commitment. Attempting to clone a chip DID is detectable via timing-side-channel proofs that a single hardware instance cannot generate two proofs simultaneously.

**Effect:** Sybil attacks require physically manufacturing more chips. The per-chip reward caps make this economically rational only at scale — and scale is what the network wants.

### 7.2 Per-Epoch Per-Chip Reward Caps

Every register has an on-chain ceiling on the TRI any single chip can earn within one epoch. Caps are set so that the top 1% of chips by performance earn no more than 100× the median chip. This prevents a single high-performance operator from monopolising a register.

Caps are recalibrated every 3 epochs (one ternary block-period tryte) based on observed network-wide proof volume.

### 7.3 Epoch Nonce Freshness

Every ZK proof must commit to the current epoch nonce sourced from the consensus beacon. Proofs referencing expired nonces (older than 2 epochs) are rejected by all verifiers. This prevents pre-computation attacks where an operator generates proofs speculatively before the epoch opens.

### 7.4 Minimum Activity Thresholds

A chip that submits fewer than 3 valid proofs in an epoch (across all modules) earns zero for that epoch, even if its individual proofs are valid. This prevents "drip" strategies where a chip submits one minimal proof per epoch to preserve its place in the reward queue while doing negligible real work.

### 7.5 Bank S: Double-Spend Prevention

Because Bank S requires human review steps (peer review, PR merge, talk co-signing), there is a risk of coordinated collusion among reviewers. Mitigations:

1. **Reviewer rotation:** The pool of eligible reviewers is randomised each cycle using a VRF seeded by the consensus beacon. No contributor can pre-select their reviewers.
2. **Conflict-of-interest exclusion:** Any reviewer who co-authored work with the contributor in the previous 24 months is excluded. Co-authorship is determined from the on-chain contribution graph.
3. **Review stakes:** Reviewers must stake a small TRI deposit when submitting a review. If the reviewed work is later found to be fraudulent (e.g., plagiarised, fabricated results), staked deposits are slashed.
4. **Retroactive audit window:** Any Bank S reward can be disputed within 180 days of issuance. A successful dispute (verified by an independent on-chain arbitration panel of 5 randomly selected chip DID holders) results in clawback of the reward and redistribution to the dispute pool.

### 7.6 Champion Register (t8): Challenge Fairness

To prevent a well-resourced operator from submitting a benchmark, collecting rewards, and then immediately breaking their own record to reset the 72-hour challenge window, the following rule applies: a chip may not hold the champion position for the same category for more than 3 consecutive challenge cycles without a successful external challenge. After 3 consecutive unchallenged cycles, the register enters a cooldown period where the chip earns at 50% of the champion rate until a new challenger appears.

### 7.7 No Retroactive Rewards

The supply schedule is fixed at genesis. No governance action can create a retroactive reward for work completed before the network's genesis block. This prevents post-hoc claims of "contributions" that predate the network.

## 10 — Protocol Invariants

The following invariants must hold at all times and are enforced by the on-chain supply manager contract:

1. **Supply ceiling:** Total minted TRI ≤ 3²⁷ at all times.  
2. **Register ceiling:** Minted TRI from register ti ≤ allocation(ti) at all times.  
3. **Proof requirement:** Every mint event is accompanied by a valid, non-expired chip ZK proof.  
4. **No pre-mine:** The genesis block mints zero TRI. The supply manager contract holds all unissued tokens in escrow until proofs are submitted.  
5. **No free distribution:** There is no code path in the supply manager contract that releases tokens to an address without a corresponding proof submission.  
6. **Ternary structure:** Bank boundaries respect the 3-subtree partition: sum(t0–t8) = 60%, sum(t9–t17) = 30%, sum(t18–t26) = 10% of total supply, verified at genesis by formal Coq proof.

## 11 — Governance and Upgradability

This document defines the canonical supply schedule at genesis. Changes to register allocations or earning criteria require:

1. A governance proposal with full specification of the proposed change.
2. A 14-day public comment period.
3. A supermajority vote (≥ 67%) of active chip DID holders weighted by average proof-verified activity over the previous 90 days.
4. A 30-day time-lock before the change takes effect (allowing dissenting chip operators to exit gracefully).

The supply ceiling (3²⁷) is immutable and cannot be changed by any governance action.
