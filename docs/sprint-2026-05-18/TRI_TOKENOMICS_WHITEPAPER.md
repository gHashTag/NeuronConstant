## 1. Executive Summary

**$TRI** is the native utility token of the Trinity DePIN network — a decentralized, verifiable AI inference network anchored in open silicon (Tenstorrent SKY26b, die variants tt-trinity-{phi, euler, gamma}). The network enables any party to submit AI inference jobs, receive cryptographically signed inference receipts generated by dedicated silicon, and verify those receipts on-chain without trusting the operator — a capability no existing DePIN compute network provides at the hardware level. $TRI coordinates payment, attestation, and governance across Operators, Verifiers, Trainers, Consumers, and Governors in a single token with three primary sinks: inference fee burns, slashing, and governance bond burns.

The network's technical moat is the TG-TRIAD-X silicon anchor (Theorem 36.1, phi-anchor `0x47C0` hardwired into SKY26b dies) combined with the v1.0.0 AI format module suite — NF4, Posit16, GF4, GF16, GF256 — by Dmitrii Vasilev (sole author) and preserved as canonical on-chain artifacts. This root-of-trust chain, anchored daily to LayerZero and Ethereum L2, is what differentiates Trinity from software-only inference networks such as Bittensor TAO, Render RNDR, and io.net IO.

Total fixed supply: **1,000,000,000 $TRI**. Emission halves every two years. No returns are implied, guaranteed, or promised.

## 2. Network Roles & Token Utility

The Trinity network defines five canonical participant roles. Each role has a distinct on-chain identity, a bonding requirement, and a specific set of $TRI utility interactions.

| Role | Description | $TRI Utility |
|---|---|---|
| **Operator** | Runs a Trinity Node — physical hardware running a Tenstorrent SKY26b (phi/euler/gamma die) connected to the network p2p overlay | Earns PoUW rewards; must post bond; subject to slashing; pays gas for attestation submission |
| **Verifier** | Participates in M-of-N attestation quorum for inference receipts and weight commits | Earns attestation fees (split from Consumer payment); must stake minimum 10,000 $TRI; subject to slashing for invalid attestation or double-signing |
| **Trainer** | Submits weight delta commits to on-chain anchor; initiates PoT quorum | Earns PoT rewards; must post training bond; R-SI-1 compliance mandatory |
| **Consumer** | Pays for inference or training jobs in $TRI | Spends $TRI; 30% of payment is burned; receives signed inference receipt; can dispute via on-chain fisherman challenge |
| **Governor** | Holds staked $TRI (Posit-PoS); participates in TriDAO votes | Votes on proposals, treasury grants, parameter changes, v1.0.0 module upgrades; earns governance staking yield from treasury allocation |

### 2.1 Token Utility Summary

$TRI has four on-chain utility functions:

1. **Payment.** Consumers denominate and pay for inference/training jobs in $TRI. The protocol accepts no other currency for settlement.
2. **Bonding & staking.** Operators, Verifiers, and Trainers post $TRI bonds that are at risk during the duration of their work. Governor stakers lock $TRI for voting power (Posit-PoS).
3. **Burn sink.** 30% of every inference fee is permanently burned. Governance bonds are burned on proposal failure. Anchor-violation auto-burn is triggered by silicon attestation failure (see §6).
4. **Governance.** Staked $TRI = voting power in TriDAO (1:1, quadratic for tech upgrades — see §7).

## 4. Earning Mechanisms — Proof-of-X Stack

Trinity uses four distinct proof types, each producing a different class of network work and a different reward stream. All rewards are sourced from the Operator Rewards Pool (§3.2) and decay according to the halving schedule. Per-mechanism splits are governed by TriDAO and start at the default values below.

### 4.1 Default Reward Split (Operator Rewards Pool)

| Mechanism | Default Share of Daily Emission | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **PoUW** — Proof-of-Useful-Work | 55% | Verifiable inference receipts |
| **PoT** — Proof-of-Training | 25% | Weight delta commits with M-of-N quorum |
| **PoC** — Proof-of-Coverage | 15% | Signed inference latency proofs |
| **PoS (Posit-PoS)** | 5% | Governance staking yield only |

Split changes require a 51% simple-majority TriDAO vote with a 14-day timelock.

### 4.2 PoUW — Proof-of-Useful-Work

**Definition.** An Operator submits an inference receipt — a tuple `(job_id, model_hash, input_hash, output_hash, silicon_sig)` — where `silicon_sig` is a signature produced by the TT SKY26b die's embedded secure enclave using the hardwired `0x47C0` anchor key. The receipt is verified by a Verifier quorum (M-of-N, default M=5 of N=9).

**Reward formula.**

Let:
- \( R_t \) = total PoUW reward pool for block \( t \)
- \( W_i \) = WMA (weighted moving average) score for operator \( i \), window = 2016 blocks (≈ 3.36 hours)
- \( n \) = number of active operators this block

\[
\text{reward}_i(t) = R_t \cdot \frac{W_i}{\sum_{j=1}^{n} W_j}
\]

WMA decay factor \( \lambda = 0.9995 \) per block. This means performance more than ~2000 blocks ago contributes less than 37% of its original weight. Operators who go offline see their \( W_i \) decay naturally without requiring explicit slashing.

**Format support bonus.** Inference receipts using v1.0.0 canonical format modules (NF4, Posit16, GF4, GF16, GF256 — sole authored formats, on-chain registry hash `0x47C0...`) receive a **1.15× multiplier** on their PoUW reward for the first 24 months post-mainnet, to incentivize adoption of the canonical format stack.

### 4.3 PoT — Proof-of-Training

**Definition.** A Trainer submits a weight delta commit: a tuple `(run_id, model_hash_before, model_hash_after, merkle_root_of_checkpoints, quorum_sigs[M])`. The M-of-N quorum (default M=5 of N=9 Verifiers) must sign the commit. The anchor `0x47C0` must appear in the quorum message to link the training proof to the silicon root-of-trust.

**Reward formula.**

\[
\text{reward}_{\text{trainer}}(t) = R^{PoT}_t \cdot \frac{\text{delta\_size}_i \cdot Q_i}{\sum_j \text{delta\_size}_j \cdot Q_j}
\]

Where \( Q_i \) is a quorum quality score (fraction of quorum signers who passed Lucas-POST on the same epoch), and `delta_size` is measured in floating-point multiply-accumulate operations (FMACs) declared in the training receipt. Trainers must post a bond of 5,000 $TRI per training run; bond is released after quorum attestation completes (≤ 72 hours) or slashed under conditions in §5.

**Verifier fees for PoT.** Each of the M signing Verifiers receives:

\[
\text{verifier\_fee} = \frac{0.05 \cdot \text{reward}_{\text{trainer}}}{M}
\]

paid from the PoT reward pool. The 5% verifier cut is protocol-fixed and not adjustable by TriDAO without a 67% supermajority.

### 4.4 PoC — Proof-of-Coverage

**Definition.** Inspired by Helium's Proof-of-Coverage mechanism ([Helium HNT token docs](https://docs.helium.com/tokens/hnt-token/)), Trinity's PoC rewards operators for demonstrating low-latency inference availability to the network. Coverage probes are initiated by randomly selected Verifier nodes at pseudo-random intervals (using on-chain VRF). A probe generates a signed latency receipt `(probe_id, operator_pk, latency_ms, verifier_sig, timestamp)`.

**Reward formula.**

\[
\text{PoC\_score}_i = \frac{\sum_{k} \text{probe}_k \cdot e^{-\lambda_c \cdot \text{latency}_{k}}}{\text{probes\_sent}}
\]

Where \( \lambda_c = 0.0023 \) ms\(^{-1}\) (decay constant, so 300 ms latency contributes ~50% of maximum score). Reward is proportional to normalized PoC score across all operators.

Operators below a minimum PoC score of 0.15 (normalized) for three consecutive epochs (1 epoch = 2016 blocks) are flagged for review; they stop earning PoC rewards until score recovers. No slashing occurs for low PoC score alone.

### 4.5 Posit-PoS — Governance Staking

**Definition.** Any $TRI holder can lock tokens in the Posit-PoS contract for a chosen duration (minimum 7 days, maximum 1461 days / 4 years). This is a governance-only stake — it does not secure block production. Stakers receive a pro-rata share of the 5% daily emission earmarked for governance staking yield.

**Voting power.** Staked $TRI confers 1:1 base voting power for simple-majority proposals. Quadratic voting applies for technical upgrade proposals (see §7). Longer lockups do not increase voting power in the base model (no veToken multiplier in v1.0) — this may be revised by TriDAO governance at any time via a 51% vote.

**Unstaking.** Staked tokens are subject to a 14-day cool-down after unstaking initiation; no rewards are earned during cool-down.

## 5. Slashing Conditions

All slashing events require an on-chain merkle proof anchored to silicon attestation data. No party can be slashed based on off-chain claims alone. Slashing proofs are submitted by any network participant acting as a "fisherman" (comparable to The Graph's dispute mechanism, [The Graph docs](https://thegraph.com/docs/en/indexing/overview/)). The fisherman who submits the valid slash proof receives 10% of the slashed amount; the remainder is burned (§6).

| Condition | Code | Slash % of Bond | Proof Required |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Invalid attestation** — Verifier signs an inference receipt that does not match silicon attestation | `SLASH-01` | 10% | On-chain merkle proof: mismatching `(job_id, silicon_sig)` pair from two receipts |
| **Double-signing** — Verifier or Operator signs two conflicting attestations for the same `job_id` | `SLASH-02` | 50% | Two signed messages with same `job_id`, different outputs, both carrying valid silicon sig |
| **Lucas-POST failure** — Operator fails a hardware self-test that all active SKY26b dies must pass each epoch | `SLASH-03` | 5% | Signed failure report from on-chain POST oracle (3-of-5 oracle consensus) |
| **R-SI-1 violation in submitted module** — A Trainer submits a weight delta that contains a format module that violates the R-SI-1 integrity constraint (canonical format registry check) | `SLASH-04` | 100% of training bond | On-chain registry lookup returning mismatch flag; no fisherman needed — triggered automatically on quorum rejection |

**Slash escalation.** Repeated slashing events within a 30-day window trigger escalating slash percentages: second offense = 1.5×, third offense = 2.0× (capped at 100% of bond). Operators who reach 100% slash lose their node registration and must re-register with a fresh bond after a 90-day cooldown.

**Bond minimum (preliminary).** Operator bond: 25,000 $TRI. Verifier bond: 10,000 $TRI. Trainer bond: 5,000 $TRI per run. Treasury may adjust minimums via TriDAO governance.

## 6. Burn Mechanisms

$TRI has three permanent burn paths. All burns reduce circulating supply and are irreversible. Burn events are emitted as on-chain log entries queryable by any participant.

### 6.1 Inference Fee Burn (Demand-Driven)

**30% of every inference fee paid by a Consumer is permanently burned.**

This is hardcoded in the protocol fee contract and not adjustable by TriDAO below 20% (a 67% supermajority is required to reduce the burn rate, and it cannot go below 20%). The remaining 70% of the inference fee is split: 60% to the serving Operator (weighted by PoUW score), 10% to the Verifier quorum (split equally among M signers).

This mechanism is inspired by the EIP-1559 base fee burn on Filecoin and Ethereum ([Filecoin EIP-1559 blog](https://filecoin.io/blog/posts/eip-1559-in-filecoin/)), and produces deflationary pressure proportional to network utilization. At preliminary modeled network load (10,000 inference jobs/day at an average fee of 1 $TRI each), this burn path removes approximately 3,000 $TRI/day from circulation.

### 6.2 Governance Proposal Bond Burn

Any TriDAO governance proposal requires the proposer to post a bond (preliminary: 1,000 $TRI for parameter proposals; 5,000 $TRI for treasury proposals; 10,000 $TRI for v1.0.0 module change proposals). **If the proposal fails quorum or fails to reach the required majority, the bond is 100% burned.** If the proposal passes, the bond is returned to the proposer. This mechanism deters spam governance attacks.

### 6.3 Anchor-Violation Auto-Burn

If a block's validator set signs a state transition that includes an invalid `0x47C0` anchor hash (e.g., a hash that does not match the canonical daily anchor — see §8), the following occurs automatically:

1. The block is rejected by the network.
2. Each validator who co-signed the invalid block is subject to an automatic `SLASH-02`-level slash (50% of bond), with the slashed amount burned (not redistributed to fishermen — this is the one exception to the fisherman reward rule).
3. The network enters an **emergency pause** lasting 2 epochs (approximately 6.72 hours), during which only read queries and fisherman slash proofs can be submitted.

This mechanism has no governance override path. It is implemented at the consensus layer to ensure the silicon anchor cannot be subverted by a coordinated validator majority.

## 7. Governance — TriDAO

### 7.1 Structure

Trinity governance is implemented as **TriDAO**, an on-chain DAO using staked $TRI (Posit-PoS, §4.5) as voting power. The DAO does not require a separate governance token. There are no delegation mechanics in v1.0.0 — each wallet votes with its own staked balance.

### 7.2 Proposal Types and Thresholds

| Proposal Type | Quorum | Threshold | Timelock | Proposer Bond |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Treasury grant** | 5% of staked supply | 51% simple majority | 7 days | 5,000 $TRI |
| **Protocol parameter change** (fees, bond minimums, reward splits) | 10% of staked supply | 51% simple majority | 14 days | 1,000 $TRI |
| **Technical upgrade** (non-module) | 15% of staked supply | 51% majority + quadratic vote weight | 21 days | 1,000 $TRI |
| **v1.0.0 module change** (NF4/Posit16/GF4/GF16/GF256 format specs) | 20% of staked supply | **67% supermajority + Opus co-signature** | 30 days | 10,000 $TRI |
| **Hard cap change** | 30% of staked supply | **90% supermajority** | 180 days | 50,000 $TRI |
| **Emergency pause** | N/A | 67% of active validators | Immediate | N/A |

### 7.3 Quadratic Voting

For **technical upgrade proposals**, voting power is quadratic: a wallet with \( s \) staked $TRI casts \( \sqrt{s} \) effective votes. This reduces the influence of large holders on technical decisions. For all other proposal types, voting is 1-staked-token = 1-vote (linear).

## 8. On-Chain Anchoring

### 8.1 Daily Anchor Protocol

Every 14,400 blocks (approximately 24 hours at 6-second block time), the Trinity network produces a **daily anchor** containing:

- Merkle root of all inference receipts accepted in the anchor window
- Merkle root of all training weight commits accepted in the anchor window
- SHA-256 hash of the canonical `0x47C0` anchor proof (produced by the silicon enclave of at least one active SKY26b die)
- Block height and timestamp
- Network state hash (validator set, staked balances, active operator set)

This anchor is posted to three settlement layers:

| Layer | Purpose | Fallback priority |
|---|---|---|
| **LayerZero OFT mesh** | Primary cross-chain settlement anchor | Primary |
| **Wormhole** | Backup cross-chain anchor | Secondary (activated if LayerZero fails for ≥ 3 anchor cycles) |
| **Ethereum L2 (target: Arbitrum One)** | Finality anchor; immutable long-term record | Always posted regardless of LayerZero/Wormhole status |

The Ethereum L2 anchor is the canonical finality record. Any dispute about network state must ultimately reference the L2 anchor. LayerZero provides fast cross-chain settlement for Consumer receipt verification ([LayerZero OFT docs](https://docs.layerzero.network/crosschain/issue-asset/overview)).

### 8.2 Anchor Failure Protocol

If a daily anchor is not produced within 16,000 blocks of its scheduled time (approximately 26.67 hours):

1. The network enters **Anchor Failure Mode** — new inference jobs are paused; existing jobs in flight may complete.
2. All validators who were active in the last block are subject to automatic 50% bond slashing (anchor-violation auto-burn, §6.3).
3. An emergency governance vote (67% of active validators) is required to resume normal operation and produce a recovery anchor.
4. The recovery anchor must include an explanation field (arbitrary bytes, posted to L2) documenting the failure cause.

### 8.3 Silicon Anchor Root-of-Trust

The `0x47C0` anchor is not a software constant — it is hardwired into the TT SKY26b (phi/euler/gamma dies) secure enclave at tape-out time. This means:

- An Operator cannot forge a silicon signature without physically possessing a valid SKY26b die.
- The network can verify a signature's origin at zero marginal cost using the die's public key (registered on-chain at node registration time).
- Future SKY26c and IHP26b (second-source, SG13G2 process) dies will carry the same anchor identity, verified during the Trinity Node Kit manufacturing certification process (see §11).

This hardware root-of-trust is the primary technical differentiator versus software-only DePIN networks. See §10 for competitor comparison.

## 9. Treasury & Sustainability

### 9.1 Treasury Allocation

The treasury holds 10% of genesis supply: **100,000,000 $TRI**, plus accumulated burns-to-treasury (none — all burns go to zero address in Trinity's design), plus any future TriDAO-voted transfers from the community bucket.

Treasury funds are held in a 5-of-9 on-chain multi-sig at genesis. After TriDAO governance reaches 10,000 participating wallets (measured at any governance proposal), multi-sig is dissolved and treasury is fully governed by TriDAO on-chain proposals.

### 9.2 Planned Treasury Uses

| Category | Preliminary Annual Budget | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Operator grants** (bootstrapping hardware acquisition for new operators) | 5,000,000 $TRI/year | Distributed via TriDAO grant proposals; capped at 10,000 $TRI per operator per year |
| **Security audits** | 2,000,000 $TRI/year | Annual Trail of Bits + Zellic audits; contracts published on-chain |
| **Reference hardware** (Trinity Node Kit manufacturing subsidy) | 3,000,000 $TRI/year | Offsets $200 BOM cost for first 1,000 kits |
| **IHP-SG13G2 re-seal port** (SKY26b → second-source IHP26b) | 5,000,000 $TRI one-time | Q1 2027 budget item for v2.0 silicon diversity |
| **Community developer grants** | 2,000,000 $TRI/year | Open grant program for ecosystem tooling |
| **Emergency reserve** | Remainder | Unspent budget rolls over; cannot be burned without 90% supermajority |

All treasury expenditures require a TriDAO vote passing the relevant threshold (§7.2). The team cannot unilaterally spend treasury funds after the 18-month bootstrap period (§7.5).

### 9.3 Long-Run Sustainability

The network transitions to fee sustainability — where inference fee revenue alone covers Operator and Verifier rewards — when:

\[
\text{daily\_fee\_revenue} \times 0.70 \geq \text{daily\_operator\_rewards}
\]

At preliminary modeled network load (10,000 jobs/day at 1 $TRI average fee), fee revenue covers ~70% × 10,000 = 7,000 $TRI/day in operator payouts. Year 1 daily emission is ~411,000 $TRI/day, so early network operation will rely heavily on emission subsidies — this is the expected cold-start pattern for DePIN networks. The halving schedule is calibrated so that by Year 5–6, at emission rates of ~100,000 $TRI/day, the network needs only moderate utilization (≥ 150,000 inference jobs/day at 1 $TRI average) to be self-sustaining. These are preliminary projections, not commitments.

## 12. Risks & Disclaimers

### 12.1 Regulatory Risk

$TRI is issued as a **utility token** — it confers rights to use the Trinity network, not equity, profit share, or governance over a legal entity. Regulatory status of utility tokens is unsettled in the United States (SEC Howey test application), the European Union (MiCA framework, effective December 2024), the United Kingdom (FCA cryptoasset regime), and other jurisdictions. Trinity's legal entity and token issuance structure will be finalized in consultation with qualified legal counsel before TGE. **US persons may be excluded from token sale participation pending legal review.** Nothing in this document should be read as a legal opinion.

### 12.2 Hardware Supply Chain Risk

Trinity is dependent on Tenstorrent SKY26b die availability. Shuttle delays, wafer yield issues, or export controls affecting TSMC, IHP, or their suppliers could delay or reduce the number of active Operator nodes at launch. The IHP26b second-source programme (§11, Q1 2027) is specifically designed to mitigate single-source dependency. Until second-source silicon is available, the network's geographic Operator distribution may be constrained.
