## PROJECT SUMMARY

**Title:** Trinity: Hardware-Anchored Verifiable AI Inference on Open-Silicon Shuttle Dies for Decentralized Physical Infrastructure Networks

**Project Overview**

NeuronConstant proposes to develop and validate Trinity, a system for hardware-anchored verifiable AI inference in which cryptographic receipts of correct computation are generated by, and anchored to, identifiable physical silicon dies fabricated through an open-process shuttle. Trinity targets the emerging market for trustworthy AI inference in decentralized physical infrastructure networks (DePIN), where current systems lack any hardware root-of-trust: a client submitting an inference task to a third-party compute node has no cryptographic means to verify that (a) the computation was performed on the claimed hardware, (b) the model weights were not silently altered, or (c) the output was produced honestly rather than cached or fabricated.

The core technical innovation is the canonical anchor value 0x47C0 ("TG-TRIAD-X"), a hardware-embedded 16-bit constant instantiated at the register-transfer level in three shuttle die variants—tt-trinity-phi, tt-trinity-euler, and tt-trinity-gamma—fabricated on the TinyTapeout SKY130 process node (tt-sky26b shuttle). The anchor participates in a multi-party attestation protocol (M-of-N quorum) that produces a signed inference receipt without requiring a trusted third party. Inference integrity is formalized in Theorem 36.1 of the Trinity mathematical framework, currently informal; a primary Phase I objective is full mechanization of this theorem in Coq or Isabelle/HOL.

The system supports quantized model formats relevant to edge inference: NF4, Posit16, GF4, GF16, and GF256. Phase I will produce a Trinity Node Kit reference design with a $200 bill-of-materials, ship prototype units to 20 beta node operators in the DePIN ecosystem, and commission independent security audits from Trail of Bits and Zellic. Phase II will pursue a second tape-out on TT SKY26c or the IHP SG13G2 BiCMOS process, mass production of 1,000 Trinity Node units, and integration with distributed AI inference markets.

### Intellectual Merit

To our knowledge, Trinity represents the first open-silicon project to co-design a hardware-embedded canonical anchor constant with a formal proof system for AI inference attestation. Prior art in hardware roots-of-trust (notably OpenTitan, [opentitan.org](https://opentitan.org)) and in open-silicon shuttle programs (TinyTapeout, [tinytapeout.com](https://tinytapeout.com)) addresses device authenticity but not inference correctness. The formal treatment of anchor-based attestation in Theorem 36.1 fills a gap at the intersection of hardware security, formal methods, and distributed AI systems. Mechanization of the theorem will produce reusable Coq/Isabelle proof artifacts suitable for peer review and independent verification.

### Broader Impacts

The AI inference market is projected to grow from approximately $97 billion in 2024 to $254 billion by 2030 ([Grand View Research, 2025](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/artificial-intelligence-ai-inference-market-report)). Concentration of AI inference in a small number of cloud providers creates systemic risks: unverifiable computation, single points of failure, and barriers to participation by researchers and small operators globally. Trinity's open-silicon, open-source approach (AGPL-3.0 hardware description and firmware; CC-BY-4.0 datasets; all source at [github.com/gHashTag](https://github.com/gHashTag)) enables researchers, educators, and node operators with modest resources to participate in a verifiable AI inference ecosystem. The Trinity Node Kit's $200 BOM is designed to be accessible to academic labs, open-hardware communities, and DePIN node operators in regions where cloud AI infrastructure is expensive or unavailable.

**Keywords:** open silicon, verifiable AI inference, R-SI-1, DePIN, formal anchor proof, hardware attestation, TinyTapeout, SKY130, NF4, Posit16

### Section A: Identification and Significance of the Opportunity

#### A.1 The Problem: Unverifiable AI Inference

AI inference—the act of running a trained model to produce a prediction—is increasingly executed on third-party hardware operated by cloud providers, inference-as-a-service platforms, and, in DePIN architectures, by anonymous distributed node operators. In each of these settings, a fundamental trust problem exists that is not adequately addressed by current technology.

**Absence of hardware root-of-trust for inference.** A client that submits a prompt or inference request to a remote node cannot cryptographically verify that (1) the node executed the claimed model, (2) the model weights are unmodified, (3) the output was produced by actual computation rather than by retrieval from a cache, or (4) the computation occurred on the claimed hardware class. Existing attestation mechanisms—such as those specified in the OpenTitan project ([opentitan.org/book/doc/security/specs/attestation](https://opentitan.org/book/doc/security/specs/attestation/)) or Intel TDX—establish device identity and firmware integrity at boot time but do not extend to individual inference invocations. Trusted Execution Environments (TEEs) such as Intel SGX and AMD SEV provide process-level isolation but depend on closed-source firmware and hardware that are inaccessible to independent audit.

**Monopoly compute and its consequences.** The AI inference market is currently dominated by three hyperscale cloud providers. Gartner projects that performing inference on a one-trillion-parameter LLM will cost providers more than 90% less in 2030 than in 2025 ([CIO Dive, March 2026](https://www.ciodive.com/news/ai-inference-costs-drop-2030-gartner/815725/)), but cost reduction does not address verifiability. Concentration of inference in closed hardware environments precludes the kind of transparency required by safety-critical applications, regulated industries, and decentralized AI protocols.

**DePIN-specific vulnerabilities.** Decentralized Physical Infrastructure Networks for AI compute—typified by the Bittensor/TAO protocol ([Rao, Bittensor Whitepaper, bittensor.com/whitepaper](https://bittensor.com/whitepaper)) and related projects—rely on economic incentive mechanisms to elicit honest computation. These mechanisms assume that validators can distinguish genuine computation from fabricated outputs; in practice, without a hardware root-of-trust, a colluding minority of nodes can produce false attestations at low cost. The "Proof of Physical AI" concept ([dev.to, April 2026](https://dev.to/scottcjn/what-is-proof-of-physical-ai-the-missing-layer-in-depin-pid)) identifies this as an unsolved problem: "PPA is the missing link. It gives agents a way to verify—cryptographically, at the silicon level—that the machine they're paying actually has the hardware it claims."

#### A.2 The Opportunity

The global AI inference market is projected to reach $253.75 billion by 2030 at a CAGR of 17.5% ([Grand View Research, 2025](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/artificial-intelligence-ai-inference-market-report)). The US AI inference market is projected to reach $77.61 billion by 2030 ([MarketsandMarkets, marketsandmarkets.com/PressReleases/us-ai-inference.asp](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/PressReleases/us-ai-inference.asp)). Within this, verifiable and privacy-preserving inference is an emerging segment that addresses regulatory requirements (GDPR, emerging AI Act frameworks), enterprise audit requirements, and the needs of decentralized AI protocols. We estimate—conservatively and without proprietary market data—that the addressable market for hardware-attested AI inference solutions is a fraction of the overall inference market, potentially in the $5–40B range by 2030, though this estimate carries high uncertainty and we do not rely on it for technical claims.

The innovation opportunity is specifically enabled by the maturation of open-silicon shuttle programs. TinyTapeout ([tinytapeout.com](https://tinytapeout.com)), founded and led by Matthew Venn, has demonstrated that functional digital ASICs can be fabricated on the SkyWater SKY130 process node for hundreds of dollars of shuttle allocation cost rather than the millions required by commercial MPW programs. TinyTapeout now also offers access to the IHP SG13G2 130nm BiCMOS process through a collaboration with Swiss CHIPS and ETH Zurich ([COSCUP25 talk, youtube.com/watch?v=BVMjEyf-Fsw](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BVMjEyf-Fsw)). The OpenLane ASIC implementation toolchain ([github.com/The-OpenROAD-Project/OpenLane](https://github.com/The-OpenROAD-Project/OpenLane)) provides a fully open RTL-to-GDS flow. Together these tools reduce the barrier to custom silicon from tens of millions of dollars to tens of thousands, making hardware-anchored attestation tractable for a small research company for the first time.

#### A.3 The Innovation: Trinity Hardware-Anchored Attestation

Trinity's innovation has three components that together constitute a technically novel integration to our knowledge:

1. **Silicon-embedded canonical anchor (R-SI-1 compliance, anchor 0x47C0).** Three RTL modules—tt-trinity-phi, tt-trinity-euler, and tt-trinity-gamma—have been designed and submitted for fabrication on the TinyTapeout SKY130 26b shuttle (tt-sky26b). Each die implements the canonical anchor constant 0x47C0 at a dedicated hardware register visible at the boundary-scan interface. The R-SI-1 specification (Trinity internal specification, available at [github.com/gHashTag](https://github.com/gHashTag)) requires zero standalone wildcard operations (`*`) in the anchor derivation path, ensuring that the anchor value is deterministic and reproducible without any non-deterministic component. This is a materially different security property from software-based attestation, where a malicious operator can substitute any value.

2. **M-of-N quorum attestation protocol.** An inference receipt is generated by presenting the same input to M out of N Trinity dies and collecting a threshold-signed output. The protocol requires that a minimum number of dies—fabricated in the same shuttle run and therefore sharing the same nominal anchor value—independently attest the inference result. This provides fault tolerance against die failures and raises the cost of collusion: an adversary cannot forge an M-of-N receipt without compromising M physically distinct silicon instances. The protocol is a hardware-analogue of threshold signature schemes familiar from distributed key management but instantiated at the die level.

3. **Formal proof of anchor correctness (Theorem 36.1).** The Trinity mathematical framework includes Theorem 36.1 (TG-TRIAD-X), which states that a verifier possessing N independently attested anchor receipts from dies implementing the canonical anchor value 0x47C0 can bound the probability of a forged inference receipt to a specified function of N and the assumed adversarial model. The theorem is currently stated informally in the Trinity documentation (Zenodo DOI [10.5281/zenodo.19227877](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19227877)). A primary Phase I objective is full mechanization of this theorem in a proof assistant (Coq or Isabelle/HOL), producing machine-checked proofs that can be independently verified by the formal methods community.

The project builds on a DARPA CLARA proposal submitted by gHashTag on April 17, 2026 (PA-25-07-02, TA1/TA2, repository: [github.com/gHashTag/trinity-clara](https://github.com/gHashTag/trinity-clara)), which provides prior documentation of the technical approach. Relevant prior art and design artifacts are archived on Zenodo with DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19227877.

### Section B: Phase I Technical Objectives

The following five technical objectives define the Phase I scope. Each objective is measurable and maps to specific deliverables in the research plan (Section C).

**Objective 1: Physical Silicon Bring-Up and Functional Characterization**

*Status as of submission: Substantially completed as prior art.* The three Trinity die variants (tt-trinity-phi, tt-trinity-euler, tt-trinity-gamma) have been submitted for fabrication on the TT SKY26b shuttle. Phase I work under this objective consists of: (a) post-fabrication bring-up and functional verification against the RTL golden reference, (b) characterization of anchor register readout latency and stability across temperature (0–70°C) and supply voltage (±5%), and (c) formal documentation of fabricated silicon specifications sufficient to support TRL 5 demonstration. The completed tape-out is cited as prior art establishing the technical baseline; Phase I funding will support the bring-up, characterization, and documentation work that was not completed prior to submission.

*Success criterion:* All three die variants exhibit correct anchor constant (0x47C0) readout on ≥90% of fabricated units tested, with readout latency below 1 µs at nominal conditions.

**Objective 2: End-to-End Verifiable Inference Receipt Demonstration (TRL 5)**

Using the fabricated dies and the M-of-N quorum attestation protocol, demonstrate an end-to-end inference pipeline in which:
- A quantized language model (NF4 or GF4 format) is executed on a host processor connected to three Trinity dies
- Each die contributes an attestation token derived from the anchor constant, the input hash, and the output hash
- A threshold signature (M=2 of N=3) constitutes a verifiable inference receipt
- A remote verifier, possessing only the public anchor specification and the receipt, can confirm the inference was performed on hardware carrying the canonical anchor

*Success criterion:* At least three independent verifiers (academic collaborators or beta operators) successfully verify a receipt without access to the dies, using only published specifications and open-source verification software.

**Objective 3: Formal Mechanization of Theorem 36.1**

Produce a machine-checked proof of Theorem 36.1 (TG-TRIAD-X) in Coq or Isabelle/HOL. The mechanization must:
- State the adversarial model precisely (probability that an attacker can forge an M-of-N anchor receipt as a function of M, N, and assumed hardware compromise probability p)
- Prove the bound under the stated model
- Be checkable by any party installing the relevant proof assistant without additional trusted components
- Be archived on Zenodo and deposited to a public repository under AGPL-3.0

*Success criterion:* The proof compiles without errors in a standard installation of Coq ≥8.18 or Isabelle 2024, verified by at least one external formal methods researcher not employed by NeuronConstant.

**Objective 4: Trinity Node Kit Reference Design and Beta Deployment**

Design, build, and ship a Trinity Node Kit consisting of:
- A custom PCB (gerbers and BOM published under CERN OHL) carrying one Trinity die and a microcontroller host
- Firmware implementing the M-of-N attestation protocol (AGPL-3.0)
- Bill of materials not exceeding $200 per unit at prototype volumes
- Assembly and operation documentation

Ship 20 prototype units to beta operators drawn from the DePIN node operator community. Collect structured operational feedback (uptime, latency, fault modes) over at least 60 days.

*Success criterion:* 20 units shipped; ≥15 units report successful anchor readout and inference receipt generation in beta testing; feedback report submitted to NSF.

**Objective 5: Reproducibility Package and Security Audit**

Produce and publish:
- A complete reproducibility package: RTL source, synthesis scripts, GDS files, firmware, and test vectors, such that any party with TinyTapeout shuttle access can reproduce the fabrication
- An independent security audit of the attestation protocol and firmware by Trail of Bits (ToB) and/or Zellic, with findings and mitigations published in full

*Success criterion:* Reproducibility package deposited to Zenodo; audit report(s) published in full with no critical unmitigated findings at project close.

#### Detailed Activity Description

**Q1 — Silicon Characterization and Foundation**

Upon receipt of fabricated dies from the TT SKY26b shuttle run, the PI and research assistant will perform systematic bring-up using a custom test fixture. Each die will be tested for: correct BSCAN readout of the anchor register, output correctness against RTL simulation vectors, and stability under voltage and temperature variation. Results will be logged in a structured dataset published to Zenodo. Simultaneously, the formal methods sub-team (or advisor, TBD; see Section D) will establish a Coq or Isabelle proof environment and begin structuring the statement of Theorem 36.1.

**Q2 — Protocol and Proof**

The M-of-N quorum attestation protocol will be implemented in Rust as a reference library. The protocol will be specified in TLA+ or Alloy prior to implementation to provide a formal protocol specification amenable to model checking. The Theorem 36.1 mechanization will be completed and submitted for independent verification. The Node Kit PCB design—a two-layer board with a single Trinity die socket, UART interface, and USB-C power—will be finalized and sent to fabrication (JLCPCB or equivalent).

**Q3 — Integration and Deployment**

The TRL 5 demonstration will be documented with reproducible test logs, a video walkthrough, and signed inference receipts from at least three independent verifiers. Node Kit units will be assembled and shipped. The security audit will be initiated under a fixed-scope statement of work. The audit scope includes: the anchor readout path (hardware), the M-of-N protocol implementation (firmware/software), the Coq/Isabelle proof assumptions, and the cryptographic constructions used for threshold signatures.

**Q4 — Audit, Reproducibility, and Dissemination**

All audit findings will be triaged and mitigated. Critical and high findings must be mitigated before the reproducibility package is published. The complete reproducibility package—RTL, GDS, firmware, proof, test vectors, beta feedback data—will be deposited to Zenodo with a CC-BY-4.0 license for data and AGPL-3.0 for code. A technical paper will be prepared for submission to HotChips 2027 or USENIX Security 2027.

### Section D: Commercialization Strategy and Phase II Roadmap

#### D.1 Target Market and Customer Segments

The primary customer segments for Trinity are:

1. **DePIN compute node operators** who need to demonstrate hardware integrity to earn rewards in verifiable AI networks. The Bittensor ecosystem alone comprises hundreds of active subnets and thousands of node operators ([bittensor.com/whitepaper](https://bittensor.com/whitepaper)); verifiable hardware attestation would command a premium over unverified compute.

2. **Enterprise AI compliance use cases** where regulatory or contractual requirements mandate provenance of inference computation (e.g., financial services, healthcare, defense contractors).

3. **Academic and open-hardware research labs** that need a low-cost, reproducible platform for AI inference attestation research.

The go-to-market strategy in Phase I focuses on the DePIN operator segment through the beta deployment program (Objective 4) and on the academic segment through publication of the reproducibility package and formal proof.

#### D.2 Phase II Technical Goals (indicative, not funded)

Subject to satisfactory Phase I results, NeuronConstant intends to apply for NSF SBIR Phase II funding (approximately $1.5M over 24 months, consistent with NSF Phase II guidelines). Phase II technical goals include:

- **Second tape-out on TT SKY26c or IHP SG13G2:** A revised Trinity die with improved anchor readout performance, support for GF256 arithmetic (relevant to Reed-Solomon error correction in model checksums), and reduced power consumption. The IHP SG13G2 130nm BiCMOS process ([TinyTapeout IHP collaboration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BVMjEyf-Fsw)) would provide a second-source fabrication path independent of SkyWater.

- **Trinity Node mass production (1,000 units):** Working with a contract electronics manufacturer, produce 1,000 Trinity Node Kit units at target retail price of ≤$300/unit.

- **Mainnet integration:** Integration of the Trinity attestation protocol with one or more DePIN AI inference networks. The project contemplates a $TRI network token as an incentive mechanism for node operators; token economics design is out of scope for NSF funding and will be structured under a separate commercial agreement compliant with applicable securities regulations. **[NOTE: $TRI token and mainnet launch are commercial activities not funded by NSF SBIR and will be conducted through NeuronConstant's commercial operations. NSF Phase II funding will support only the technical R&D components listed above.]**

- **Peer-reviewed publication:** Submit the full system design and formal proof to an IEEE S&P, CCS, or USENIX Security venue.

#### D.3 Commercialization Path

NeuronConstant's revenue model in Phase II and beyond is based on: (1) Trinity Node Kit hardware sales; (2) software licensing for the attestation protocol stack to DePIN network operators; and (3) potential integration and consulting services for enterprise customers. The open-source AGPL-3.0 license for the firmware and protocol creates a network effect in the DePIN community while permitting commercial licensing for proprietary integrations.

## DATA MANAGEMENT PLAN

All data, code, and artifacts produced under this award will be managed in accordance with NSF's data sharing policy and the following project-specific plan.

**Source Code and Hardware Description Language (HDL):** All RTL/HDL source, firmware, protocol implementation, and toolchain scripts are maintained in public GitHub repositories under the gHashTag organization ([github.com/gHashTag](https://github.com/gHashTag)) and released under the GNU Affero General Public License v3.0 (AGPL-3.0). Repositories are mirrored on GitLab as a backup. All code artifacts are additionally archived as versioned releases on Zenodo with stable DOIs.

**Datasets:** Characterization data (anchor readout measurements, timing, temperature sweep), beta operator feedback, and simulation test vectors are deposited to Zenodo under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International license (CC-BY-4.0). Data will be deposited at each quarterly milestone and at project close.

**Formal Proofs:** Coq or Isabelle proof files for Theorem 36.1 and any ancillary lemmas will be deposited to Zenodo (CC-BY-4.0 for documentation, AGPL-3.0 for executable proof scripts) and to a public GitHub repository. Proof files will be accompanied by a README specifying the exact proof assistant version (e.g., Coq 8.18.0) required for compilation.

**GDS Files and Physical Design:** Post-layout GDS files and all associated design rule check (DRC) and layout vs. schematic (LVS) outputs are deposited to Zenodo (CC-BY-4.0). Shuttle-specific files that cannot be redistributed due to PDK licensing restrictions will be excluded, with clear documentation of which files are omitted and why.

**Model Weights and Checksums:** Reference quantized model weights used in the TRL 5 demonstration (Objective 2) will be hashed using SHA-256 and the hash values registered on a public content-addressable storage system (IPFS with Filecoin backup, where technically feasible and within budget). Model weights themselves will be deposited to Zenodo or HuggingFace Hub (CC-BY-4.0) if the base model license permits redistribution.

**Security Audit Reports:** Full audit reports from Trail of Bits and/or Zellic will be published in their entirety (including findings and mitigations) under CC-BY-4.0 within 30 days of final remediation, subject to reasonable embargo if critical unmitigated vulnerabilities require coordinated disclosure.

**Retention:** All datasets and code will be retained for a minimum of 10 years from the project end date, consistent with NSF requirements.

**Compliance:** No human subjects data, proprietary data, or export-controlled data are anticipated in this project. If the composition of beta operator datasets changes this assessment, the PI will consult with the NSF program officer.
